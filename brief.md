Overview
A national digital login service lets citizens use one identity to access multiple government websites and mobile services. Software standards establish how participating services confirm identity and request access without sharing passwords. Artificial intelligence can identify unusual login activity, but its decisions need testing, oversight, and a route for correcting mistakes. The Android app must respect device permissions and ask for sensitive access only when needed. Networking protections such as DNS over HTTPS reduce exposure of domain lookups, although they do not make the entire connection anonymous. Cybersecurity controls must resist fake login pages and stolen credentials. Finally, data governance determines how identity records and login histories are collected, protected, retained, and handled under Oman's applicable rules. These six fields must be designed together to deliver secure, usable access.
Software
OAuth 2.0 is an authorization framework: it lets an application obtain limited access to protected resources, normally using access tokens. It does not, by itself, define a reliable user-login result. OpenID Connect (OIDC) adds an identity layer on top of OAuth 2.0 for authentication. Its ID token conveys verified information about the signed-in user. For this service, use OIDC for citizen login and OAuth-based access tokens for authorized requests to government APIs.
Resource: OpenID Connect Core 1.0 incorporating errata set 2 - https://openid.net/specs/openid-connect-core-1_0-35.html
AI
Before deployment, apply the NIST AI Risk Management Framework's trustworthiness criteria. Test whether suspicious-login predictions are accurate and reliable, particularly for legitimate citizens whose behavior differs from training data. Manage unfair bias, false lockouts, privacy risks from login histories, adversarial manipulation, and security failures. Document decisions and explanations, assign accountability, and monitor the model after release. Provide human review and an appeal path when automated flags could deny access to essential government services.
Resource: AI Risks and Trustworthiness - https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/3-sec-characteristics/
Mobile
Android separates ordinary permissions from runtime, or dangerous, permissions that expose restricted data or actions. The login app cannot assume access to contacts, location, camera, or microphone; it must check and request the relevant runtime permission before using a protected feature. It should ask in context, when the citizen starts that feature, rather than on first launch. If permission is denied, the app should avoid that restricted access and offer an alternative where possible.
Resource: Permissions on Android - https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/permissions/overview
